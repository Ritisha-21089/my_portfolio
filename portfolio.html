Research

RecipeNER-FG

A fine-grained NER corpus for recipe ingredients, and a ten-model benchmark over it.

Application of Deep Learning for Named Entity Recognition in Recipes

B.Tech thesis · Complex Systems Laboratory, IIIT-Delhi · 2024–2025

Ingredient phrases look simple and aren't. "2 cups finely chopped roma tomatoes, divided" packs a quantity, a unit, a state, a modifier, a cultivar-level name and a usage note into six words, and coarse NER schemas collapse most of that into a single INGREDIENT span. The corpus uses an 8-tag fine-grained schema that keeps those distinctions.

Phrases were sampled from RecipeDB using spherical k-means over TF-IDF vectors so the annotated set spans the lexical space rather than over-representing common ingredients. Quality was measured, not asserted: a 250-phrase doubly annotated subset reached κ = 0.90, with formal tag definitions and a written adjudication process for boundary cases.

Ten architectures were benchmarked under identical conditions. Beyond the headline number, the more useful contribution is a negative one — WordNet synonym augmentation hurt every single model, consistently, by disrupting the distributional cues that mark entity boundaries. It's documented reproducibly so nobody else has to spend a month rediscovering it.
